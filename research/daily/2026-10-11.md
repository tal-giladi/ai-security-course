### C-20261011-01 · AI-content watermarking topic (opened 2026-10-10) upgraded to A: SemField ships an open-source, baseline-beating text-watermark defense; MARC adds a third (audio) data point — same arXiv batch, three modalities, attack+defense both measured

- **Date published:** 2026-10-08 (both papers, v1)

Code: https://github.com/declare-lab/SemField. Marked "under review and WIP." MARC embeds a multi-bit payload in a codec-aware token-cluster space for autoregressive audio generation, reporting 97.3% average bit-extraction accuracy on unmodified watermarked audio and claimed (no abstract-page numbers) survival of codec and overwriting attacks; no code confirmed.

### C-20261011-02 · ReSI: a recursive red-team-then-align training loop cuts mean X-Teaming jailbreak ASR from 86.01% to 31.45% across 4 dense/MoE models while gating every update on capability retention — new "automated safety-training loop" sub-topic

- **Class:** B
- **Date discovered:** 2026-10-11
- **Date published:** 2026-10-08 (v1)
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12233
- **Organization/researchers:** Jingnan Zheng, Dongcheng Zhang, Yi Zhang, Ming Zhang, Qiaosheng Zhang, Youbang Sun, An Zhang, Xiangnan He, Tat-Seng Chua, Xia Hu, Bowen Zhou, Chaochao Lu, Xiang Wang (affiliations not independently re-verified this run; several of these names are associated with NUS/USTC-area academic groups in prior published work, not confirmed here).
- **Category:** defense/guardrail (instruction-hierarchy / safety-alignment training loop; closest protocol-listed area is "instruction-hierarchy training, certified/robust methods")
- **What changed:** The course already teaches instruction-hierarchy training as a defense mechanism (`papers/02-wallace-instruction-hierarchy.md`), but has no coverage of an automated, iterative "red-team your own current model, fine-tune on what broke it, keep only Pareto-improving updates, repeat" training loop. ReSI proposes exactly this as a named, repeatable procedure rather than a one-shot alignment pass.
- **Technical summary:** Each round, ReSI runs diverse red-teaming methods against the current model, builds training recipes from what succeeded, and keeps only the update with the largest safety gain that passes a Pareto gate on capability retention; that update becomes next round's target. Evaluated on 4 dense and mixture-of-experts models: mean attack success rate under the X-Teaming red-teaming method fell from 86.01% to 31.45%, below a stated 56.69% for GPT-5.6-Luna (that comparison figure is as reported by this paper, not independently verified against GPT-5.6-Luna's own disclosures — flagged as such). The authors report ReSI matches or exceeds evaluated frontier models on in- and out-of-distribution safety benchmarks and beats alignment baselines on nearly all safety evaluations while largely preserving general capability.
- **Why it might matter:** A measured defense with an explicit capability-retention cost gate (exactly the course's `.callout.guarantee` pattern: what the Pareto gate guarantees for capability vs. what it costs in safety-gain speed) against a named, reproducible red-teaming method (X-Teaming), from a reasonably large multi-author team, in an area (automated/iterative safety training) the course has not yet taught as its own mechanism distinct from one-shot instruction-hierarchy training.
- **Evidence of adoption:** None stated (preprint, 3 days old); not a vendor claim. The GPT-5.6-Luna comparison number is this paper's own evaluation of that model, not GPT-5.6-Luna's own report — treat as this-paper's-claim, not vendor claim, but also not independently cross-checked this run.
- **Major organizations using it:** none stated.
- **Open-source implementation:** not stated/confirmed on the abstract page — check the full PDF.
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12233
- **Code:** none confirmed
- **Relationship to existing course material:** `lookup "instruction hierarchy"` finds the course's existing Wallace et al. instruction-hierarchy-training lesson; `lookup "recursive safety improvement"`, `lookup "X-Teaming"`, and `lookup "Pareto safety capability"` found no existing registry/course match — new sub-topic (iterative red-team-then-align loop), sibling to but distinct from one-shot instruction-hierarchy training.
- **Potential course lesson:** Possible module extension pairing with the instruction-hierarchy lesson: implement a toy "red-team → fine-tune → Pareto-gate → repeat" loop against a small model and a handful of scripted attack templates, measuring ASR and a capability proxy metric across rounds to make the safety/capability trade-off concrete.
- **Confidence:** medium (concrete multi-model ASR numbers and an explicit capability-retention gate, reasonably large author list, but single preprint, 3 days old, no independent replication, no code confirmed, and the frontier-model comparison number is self-evaluated by the paper rather than vendor-reported).
- **Recommendation:** monitor for the weekly review; worth a second look once the full PDF's capability-retention metric and any code release can be checked, and once the X-Teaming comparison methodology is confirmed as fair/reproducible.
